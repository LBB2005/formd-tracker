# What six years of SEC private-fundraising filings show

<p class="dek">Every startup raise and venture fund filed on Form D since 2019, cleaned up into single raises, sorted into startups, funds and SPVs, and tagged for AI. Four things stood out. Each links to the page with the live numbers.</p>

<div class="card">

### 1. The biggest AI companies are mostly invisible

More than half of the well-known SF AI companies I checked never filed a Form D under their own name. OpenAI and Anthropic don't. Dozens of SPVs buying their shares do. You can see investor demand for these companies in the data, but not the companies themselves.

<a href="./coverage">Coverage gap →</a>

</div>

<div class="card">

### 2. AI has taken over SF startup money, and it isn't just xAI

AI companies raised 75% of SF startup dollars in 2026. xAI alone was 40%, but take it out and AI is still 58%: Databricks, Baseten, Harvey and many smaller companies make up the rest.

<a href="./">Overview →</a> · <a href="./ai-raises">AI raises →</a>

</div>

<div class="card">

### 3. The "VC fund boom" is partly an SPV boom

Two-thirds of "new venture fund" filings in the US are really single-deal SPVs. Counting raw filings overstates new funds by about 3x nationally and 1.4–1.6x in SF and LA.

<a href="./funds">New funds →</a>

</div>

<div class="card">

### 4. First-time managers still dominate

About three in four new VC funds are a manager's first, second or third fund, and that share has barely moved since 2019.

</div>
